[[[ To any NSA and FBI agents reading my email: please consider    ]]]

  > Of course, since GNU Emacs is built to give users freedom to use, 
  > modify, and extend it as they see fit, there's nothing we could do to 
  > prevent someone from integrating LLMs into their Emacs. However, that 
  > doesn't mean we need to embrace it ourselves.

That is true, of course -- but it slightly mis-states the issue at
hand.  To correct slightly the point Stefan made in his reply, this
text quoted above changes the issue from "stand against nonfree
software" to "stand against LLMs."

We don't reject LLMs on principle.  We do reject freedom-denying LLMs
on principle, along with other freedom-denying computing technology.
Given a useful, free LLM, we would have no a priori objection to it.

We are temporarily rejecting code made by LLMs, but that is not a
moral judgment.  It is because we do not know how that affects
copylefting the code.
