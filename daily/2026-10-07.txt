  > On the other hand, Gmail is so popular that it's not as though we're 
  > *promoting* it to users who might not be aware. For better or worse, 
  > everyone who'd read the Gnus manual already knows about Gmail.

That is true.  "Pretend Gmail does not exist" is not obligatory,
and I would guess it is not a very effective way of fighting back.

  > However, I don't see anything in the manual about 
  > configuring to use OAuth or "less-secure" authentication, so it's not as 
  > helpful for this goal as it could be.

Since (according to your message) configuring that way involves
running some nonfree software, we have to be careful how to present
that.  Perhaps, "There is a way you can run some nonfree software one
last time so as to cut your ties with unjust Gmail authentication."

There is a crucial moral difference between (1) "I choose nasty
practice A because it is not as nasty as practice B", and (2) "I will
do X _once_ to cut the ties that compel me to accept practice Y."  If
you are willing to accept pattern (1) as valid, that willingness can
be used to maneuver you into rationalizing any injustice, simply by
making you choose between that one and something worse.  Not so for
(2).
