I want to include in the elisp version, I *could* do that by asking an agent

This is important in the context of agent-assisted coding, because, at
least in semi-recent practice, it is unfortunately common for coding
agents to "fix problems" by starting over, at least for large sections of
a codebase.

to address all, most, or even necessarily any changes to the elisp
version by reverting partially or wholly to the C version; the poster
was instead saying that, in the case that there is a specific change
to the C code, the problem of "rewriting large chunks of the code"
(a current version of the CADT problem) can be avoided.

This distinction has given rise to different peoples having distinct
conversations "past" one another, an unfortunately common
occurrence, especially in on-line conversations. I hope that my
addition will help defray that; if I am unsuccessful, please
accept my apologies to all involved for adding to the volume.

Abstractly speaking, there may be multiple methods to achieve a goal.
For software freedom we are concerned with the existence of plausible
method to achieve the goal which doesn't depend on non-free software.

that doesn't matter.  Do we at least agree on that?

non-free software to change the software or even to backport changes.

comparatively more time -- the decision of a maintainer to take this
