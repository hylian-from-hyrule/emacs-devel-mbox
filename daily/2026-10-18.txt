Fixed by the 1st attached patch, which simplifies the code a tiny bit.

> are we now
> supposed to get rid of all the variable-length arrays in Emacs? why?
Although it's not essential to remove VLAs, it improves portability and makes static analyzers less nervous. VLAs are a notorious source of low-level attacks on C programs, and I suspect that's why Microsoft pushed back on them and got them removed (OK, well, made them optional) in recent editions of the C standard. Even platforms that support VLAs often look askance at them.

For the abovementioned warnings, VLAs were not particularly useful or efficient, and were easy to remove.

You're right `mouse-event-p` isn't needed, but I do like the clarity of
"if this is a mouse-event, we may need to temporarily move point".  And
ctually, I changed my mind and went with a simpler version I found in
the meantime: no shady `refactor--1` internal interactive, and no
"call-at-mouse" wrapper.  `refactor` keeps its (BEG END RKIND
INTERACTIVE) signature and its interactive spec deals with mouse events.
That spec turned out quite short, since margin and mode-line clicks just
mean "point":

  (interactive
   (let ((posn (and (mouse-event-p last-nonmenu-event)
                    (event-start last-nonmenu-event))))
     (when posn (select-window (posn-window posn)))
     `(,@(save-excursion
           ;; Non-text-area clicks (`posn-area' non-nil) already assume
           ;; correct point, but text area clicks are likely on Flymake
           ;; some diagnostic and need moving before `refactor--bounds'.
           (when (and posn (null (posn-area posn)))
             (goto-char (posn-point posn)))
           (refactor--bounds))
       ,(and current-prefix-arg ...)
       t)))

I like that `refactor` is now one function, one name. (refactor BEG END)
stays a usable Lisp entry point returning the actions.  Tests and
backend authors can use that. Also the kind-specific commands have the
same signature again and `eglot-code-actions` can be a plain obsolete
alias again.
