My hunch is that we'll have to start thinking along other lines.

GNU is, at its core a "social" project, in the sense that it tries
to enable people to exercise their right to freedom. It focuses
very much on the individual, and uses copyright (a clever hack!)
to make sure those freedoms aren't hogged by some.

This has served us outstandingly for the last 30 years.

Alas, since around the 2000s, with the advent of surveillance
capitalism, those who make their profit by curtailing (software)
freedom have been learning how to do that by targeting not so
much the individuals, as their social interactions.

The free software movement has, in part, reacted to that: the
critique of (non-free) SASS, the AGPL, etc are witnesses to this
reaction.

So-called "AI" (as it is being rolled out currently) is just the
last, extreme acceleration of the above process (target social
interactions, not individuals).

In my view, one of the "soft" targets of free software now in
peril is "software as a conversation between humans", which I
think lies at the core of software's Freedom 1 (study) and
Freedom 3 (improve). I sure want to know if I'm talking to
a human or a machine (especially if the latter is controlled
by a huge company).

Cheers
-- 
tomás
