The fact that `A-2.el` happens to end up in the `load-path` is just an
"internal detail".

If they want to use A-2 as well, they should install it (which
presumably would add a symlink from directory `A-2` to directory `A`
and then look at the headers of `A-2.el`, ...).

Of course, we could be more proactive: *after* installing `A` (and hence
consulting the headers of `A.el`), we could scan the other files and ask
the users whether they also want to install those.

[ Note also that I've seen files `FOO.el` with headers like
  `Package-Requires:` even when there's no intention to make a package
  FOO out of it, so the scan risks finding packages that don't exist.  ]

Please take a look at the source code of vibe-spell.  I am half way

the average ELPA submission.

https://raw.githubusercontent.com/astoff/vibe-spell/refs/heads/main/vibe-spell.el

The "preferred form" question is a category error here, since the
translation is one between two forms of equal standing and not
comparable to machine-code output.  My guess is that the LLM would most
probably be able to recover a fully functional C++ version from the
Elisp code without much more difficulty in a way that a even a
decompiler wouldn't be able to do.

doesn't result in so-called "slop" (which I understand to be some
horrible plague that nobody must ever come into contact with) and would
most probably never have been attempted without the availability of such
tools.

changeset, we always have a lot of eyes will on it, because we want to
make sure such changes are in the right direction, well designed and
maintainable in the long run.  And because Emacs is a very complex
program with a lot of subtle aspects that no single person knows
enough about, we usually ask everyone who is an expert in some parts
of Emacs to review such patches.

participate in the relevant discussions.  And because these cases are
rare in Emacs, it makes it possible for all those people to be part of
the discussions and provide valuable inputs and ides.

very different dynamics: they still frequently add major features,
they are under a constant pressure to put out significant features and
improvements in order not to "lose the race" to competing packages and
products, etc. etc. -- all of these things are very different from our
development dynamics.

I'm not saying the danger doesn't exist, I'm saying that our
development dynamics and practices make it significantly smaller
because many factors that urge people to use LLM-generated code more
and more don't exist here.

> 
> Historically, C-c was reserved for users' personal commands.

That's not accurate.  The ELisp manual says about this:

     • Don't define ‘C-c LETTER’ as a key in Lisp programs.  Sequences
       consisting of ‘C-c’ and a letter (either upper or lower case; ASCII
       or non-ASCII) are reserved for users; they are the *only* sequences
       reserved for users, so do not block them.

       Changing all the Emacs major modes to respect this convention was a
       lot of work; abandoning this convention would make that work go to
       waste, and inconvenience users.  Please comply with it.

     • Function keys <F5> through <F9> without modifier keys are also
       reserved for users to define.

     • Sequences consisting of ‘C-c’ followed by a control character or a
       digit are reserved for major modes.

     • Sequences consisting of ‘C-c’ followed by ‘{’, ‘}’, ‘<’, ‘>’, ‘:’
       or ‘;’ are also reserved for major modes.

     • Sequences consisting of ‘C-c’ followed by any other ASCII
       punctuation or symbol character are allocated for minor modes.
       Using them in a major mode is not absolutely prohibited, but if you
       do that, the major mode binding may be shadowed from time to time
       by minor modes.

So only "C-c <LETTER>" sequences are reserved for users.

> Emacs should have better default key bindings. This is not a feature;
> it is a lack of respect for the keyboard.
> 
> For example, in python.el in Emacs 31.1. [[file:/usr/share/emacs/31.1/lisp/progmodes/python.el::(defvar-keymap python-base-mode-map]]
> 
> This begins with C-p and C-b, which cause the most confusion for
> beginners and slow down not-very-smart long-time users.

This is in agreement with the above guidelines for major modes.

> My suggestions:
>  1) Don't use C-c in major modes.
>  2) Research some soft recommendations that will arrange keybinds
>  more standardly across built-in modes.
>  3) (optional) Get rid of C-p and C-b by clearly suggesting to users
>  from the first manual that they choose their own keys.
>  Also, C-u and C-h should more conventionally follow *nix.

I think it's too late to change these conventions that are in place
for several decades, and generations of Emacs users have them burned
into their muscle memories.  We'd be lynched if we change such basic
bindings.
