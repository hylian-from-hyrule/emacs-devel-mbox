Anyone who knows something about Emacs development and maintenance for
the past decade or two can immediately see that the projects described
in that blog are very different from what happens in Emacs nowadays.
While the dangers described there are not unimaginable in Emacs, there
are several factors that make them highly improbable.  One of them,
perhaps the most important one, is that Emacs's codebase is very old
and very stable, and that significantly affects the kinds of changes
are being submitted most of the time, in ways that make those dangers
much less likely to happen here.  If you carefully read what's
described in those blogs, you will clearly see that the projects and
the development activities they talk about are different in many
important aspects.

Bottom line: we should be careful when we learn from what happened in
other projects, certainly when the conclusions are so radical.

in particular seemed a non sequitur to your reply and (as you noted at

pushy in a bug or two on the list - but LLMs make it all the easier to

Since this is but a single example, it's fair to say I'm making

these projects become distrustful of outsiders in proportion to the rise 
of those issues. Hopefully we can avoid that here (not just for you, but 
for anyone else who responds to bug reports).

> If you mean encouraging people to do things themselves so they learn
> better and faster, no one in their right minds will object to that.
> Nor would comments to that effect in a discussion of a submission be
> out of place, IMO.  But that is a very far cry from what the OP said
> when he started this thread, and a very-very-VERY far cry from forking

That's good to hear. I think additionally making it clear that a project 
*expects* people to do things themselves to assist learning will help 
attract exactly the sort of people who want to learn and - in at least a 
few cases - step up to help maintain Emacs in the future.

These are of course only my views, since I'm just someone worried about 
the trajectory of other F/OSS projects who hopes Emacs can avoid the 
same. I don't relish forking, and view the possible fork only as a 
signpost that there will be *some* version of Emacs for those who want a 
community that prioritizes learning and developing our craft.

> This is again about those people who cannot use LLMs wisely.  It
> doesn't affect the Emacs maintainers, who need to deal with such
> attitude already, and it doesn't affect Emacs and its code.  I don't
> see why we should be responsible for people who are unwise enough to
> hurt their own abilities, and I definitely don't see how that could be
> stopped by refusing to accept LLM-generated code or by forking Emacs.

My concern is with the level of discipline needed to maintain this 
wisdom. While a few people may be able to maintain strict personal 
boundaries on how they use LLMs, their design encourages ever-greater 
adoption. (How much this is intentional, I don't know.) I'd like to 
think of myself as particularly disciplined, but I've seen enough cases 
of others going overboard that I don't want to risk it.

Currently, Emacs has fairly strict community-set limits due to the 
copyright situation. That's done much to protect against these issues 
here, and I think it would be best to avoid relaxing these limits *even 
if* the FSF concluded that LLMs don't undermine copyleft.

> I see it as a greatly exaggerated reaction to problems that are
> largely if not completely unrelated to Emacs and its development.
> Where they could be relevant, they didn't yet happen, and the fears
> (at least those I've seen written so far) are based on basic mistrust
> of our abilities and intentions as Emacs maintainers.

I don't mean this as a criticism of any of the maintainers' abilities or 
intentions (past or present). Rather, I fear that relaxing our LLM 
policies will make it harder for maintainers to apply their (our?) 
abilities and intentions to keeping Emacs on the same positive road it's 
been on for so long.

Put another way: I think our existing policies and maintainership have 
been very good. I'm suggesting that we extend the reasoning for our ban 
on LLM contributions from the temporary copyleft dispute to include 
protecting against the negative social effects seen in other projects. 
That's all, really.

In particular, I'm not suggesting Emacs immediately adopt any new 
policies. While I'm concerned about the LLM-assisted bug reports, 
they're certainly not a crisis yet, so there's no need to rush to make a 
change. For that case, all I hope for is that people on Debbugs keep in 
mind that have to deal with LLM bug spam, so we should stay alert in 
case that happens here.
